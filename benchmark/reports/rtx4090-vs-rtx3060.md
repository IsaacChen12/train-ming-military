## Key Findings

### 1. A model that fits fully in VRAM on both cards consistently lost ~35% generation speed
`qwen3.5:9b` (6.6GB), `gemma4:12b` (7.6GB), and `qwen2.5` 7.6B (4.7GB) all fit with room to spare in 16GB *and* 12GB — no offload needed on either machine. All three lost roughly a third of their generation speed on Machine B, and tightly clustered at that: **-34.8%, -35.7%, -37.7%**. Three independent data points landing within 3 points of each other is a strong signal this is a stable, model-independent hardware ratio, not per-model noise. Since VRAM headroom isn't the bottleneck for any of them, the gap comes down to raw GPU throughput/memory bandwidth: the 4090 Laptop GPU simply processes tokens roughly 35% faster than the desktop 3060 for anything that fits in VRAM, independent of offload.

### 2. Prompt-eval speed is far more sensitive to the GPU gap than generation speed
Generation (decoding one token at a time) is memory-bandwidth-bound and dropped ~28-38% across models. Prompt evaluation (processing the whole input in parallel) is compute-bound and dropped much harder — **-59.4%** for `gemma4:12b` and up to **-67.3%** for `qwen3.5:9b`. If your workload is prompt-heavy (e.g. RAG with large retrieved context), the effective slowdown on the weaker GPU will be worse than the tok/s numbers alone suggest.

### 3. The 35B MoE model degraded *less*, proportionally, than every fully-resident model
Counter-intuitively, `qwen3.6:35b` — which needs CPU offload on both machines — only dropped 27.6%, less than all three smaller models that fit entirely in VRAM (~35-38%). Once a workload is already bottlenecked by CPU↔GPU offload and CPU compute for a large share of layers, the raw GPU speed difference between the two cards matters proportionally less. MoE's sparse activation (only a fraction of the 35B parameters run per token) likely compounds this by reducing dependence on either GPU's raw throughput.

### 4. No OOM on either machine for the 35B model, despite 22-23GB > both cards' VRAM
Ollama's CPU offload handled the overflow on both the 16GB and 12GB cards without failure — only `gemma4:26b` (18GB, tested on Machine A only) actually OOM'd, likely due to how that specific model's memory layout interacts with offload rather than VRAM size alone.

### 5. Model selection cannot be transferred between machines as-is
On Machine A, `qwen3.5:9b` was the clear #2 by a wide margin behind a 7.6B model, with `qwen3.6:35b` well behind. On Machine B, `qwen2.5:7b` is fastest, `qwen3.5:9b` is 2nd, and `qwen3.6:35b` is closer to `qwen3.5:9b` in relative terms than it was on Machine A. Absolute rankings held for the two shared models here, but the *margins* shifted enough that a "good enough" choice on one machine may be a bottleneck on another — always re-benchmark rather than assume.

## Practical takeaway

- If a model already fits comfortably in VRAM on your target machine, don't assume its speed transfers from a benchmark on different hardware — expect a real gap driven by raw GPU compute, not just memory capacity.
- Prompt-heavy workloads (RAG) are hit harder by a GPU downgrade than chat-style short-prompt workloads.
- A model requiring CPU offload is more "hardware-portable" in relative terms than a model that fits entirely in VRAM, precisely because offload is already the bottleneck.
